How to calculate molar mass

  1. Count the atoms of each element in the formula. A subscript after parentheses multiplies everything inside: Ca(OH)₂ has 2 O and 2 H.
  2. Multiply each count by the element’s standard atomic weight (in g/mol).
  3. Add the products. The sum is the molar mass: the mass in grams of one mole of the substance.

Worked example: sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄

ElementAtoms × atomic weightg/molMass %
H2 × 1.0082.0162.06 %
S1 × 32.0632.0632.69 %
O4 × 15.99963.99665.25 %
Molar mass of H₂SO₄98.072 g/mol

The mass percent of each element is its contribution divided by the molar mass: 63.996 / 98.072 = 65.25 % oxygen.

Questions.

What is molar mass?
The mass of one mole of a substance, in grams per mole (g/mol). It has the same number as the relative formula mass: water, H₂O, is 18.015 g/mol.
Is the molar mass calculator free?
Yes. The molar mass and the composition by element are free in the app, with no ads and no account.
Does it work with hydrates and complex ions?
Yes: write CuSO4·5H2O, K4[Fe(CN)6] or SO4^2- and the app reads the coefficient, the brackets and the charge.
